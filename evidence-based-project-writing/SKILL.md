# evidence-based-project-writing

Write project material that accurately reflects the work and gives readers the context they need.

Research and evidence

- Read the requested source files and inspect the relevant implementation. For project stories, use commit history or dated materials when requested and available.
- Base claims on supplied material. Do not invent motivations, outcomes, metrics, users, technical decisions, dates, or capabilities.
- Keep documented facts distinct from interpretation. If evidence is missing or conflicting, qualify the statement or leave it out.
- Respect attribution instructions, privacy boundaries, and explicit exclusions. Preserve content the user says must remain unchanged.

Writing

- Follow the project’s existing voice, structure, and terminology where appropriate. Use the `speak` skill for general voice and educational writing when available.
- Explain the product, the problem, decisions, implementation, and resulting behaviour with concrete details. Avoid generic praise or a feature list that does not explain why the work matters.
- Keep documentation aligned with the current implementation. Update related references only when the requested change makes them inaccurate.
- Use headings, lists, tables, and examples only when they improve understanding. Do not pad the material with repeated introductions or summaries.
